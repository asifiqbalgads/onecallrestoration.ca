The first call, then what

Five steps from
emergency to hand-over.

Same sequence on a ceiling leak at 2am or a hail-damaged roof on a Friday evening — so nobody has to improvise.

  1. Emergency intake

    You call the 24/7 line. We triage the hazard, tell you what to isolate before we arrive, and dispatch a crew.

    Minute one

  2. Site assessment

    Moisture mapping, visibility and damage scope on site, with an explanation of what we found in plain language.

    On arrival

  3. Mitigation & drying

    Water extraction, board-up, containment, air movers and dehumidifiers running — plus equipment on a schedule.

    Day 1–3

  4. Documentation

    Daily readings, photos and job notes assembled into records you can hand straight to your insurer or board.

    Daily

  5. Rebuild & close-out

    Drywall, paint, ceilings, finishes and final walk-through — one signed-off scope, not a stack of sub-trades.

    Hand-over

Before you call

Questions we answer daily.

The 24/7 emergency line is the dispatch channel, day or night. Rapid-response is the whole point of the team: we triage by phone, tell you what to isolate safely, and get a crew moving to the site. If you are mid-flood, call rather than fill in a form.

Our service pages are written around commercial restoration — offices, retail, institutional and multi-unit buildings — and that is where our equipment and scheduling are strongest. Reviewers also describe condo and basement work, so tell us the situation and we will say quickly whether we are the right fit.

Yes. Documentation is part of the job: moisture readings, equipment logs, photographs and scope notes, kept up to date so owners, property managers and adjusters are working from the same record. Reviewers repeatedly highlight that One Call keeps owners and companies informed, including on delays.

The rebuild stage after mitigation: framing, drywall and ceilings, insulation, paint, trim and finishes, plus the structural repairs that go with fire, storm or water losses. One team holds the schedule instead of your property manager chasing separate trades.

Yes — asbestos and mould abatement is listed as one of our core services alongside fire, water and storm restoration. That work starts with containment and controlled access before affected materials are removed and the area is cleaned for re-occupancy.

Keep people clear of the affected area, stop the source if it is safe to do so, switch off electricity to wet zones at the panel, and move uncovered contents to a dry area if you can do it without risk.
